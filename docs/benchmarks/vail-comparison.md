# Comparison with Vail decoder

Measured on 2026-10-17 against Vail revision
[`ffb61d5`](https://github.com/TheRealKamisama/vail-decoder/tree/ffb61d5bd7f010cedeb291e440c1781052c24c48).
The baseline reproduces its [published comparison](https://github.com/TheRealKamisama/vail-decoder/blob/ffb61d5bd7f010cedeb291e440c1781052c24c48/docs/libmorsedec-comparison.md).
The current decoder has slightly lower overall CER on that grid and lower CER
on an additional synthetic grid. This is an aggregate result, not a claim to
win every condition or every radio recording.

| Evaluation | Cases | Vail mean CER | libmorsedec baseline | libmorsedec current |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Vail's published synthetic grid | 464 | 3.721% | 10.439% | **3.606%** |
| Additional messages, speeds and pitches | 120 | 6.112% | Not measured | **4.478%** |

The published grid contains 18,940 normalized reference characters. Vail made
716 edit errors, baseline libmorsedec 1,649, and current libmorsedec 664. Pooled
CER is therefore 3.780%, 8.706%, and 3.506%, respectively. The headline follows
Vail's convention of averaging per-case CER, which gives short messages more
weight than pooled CER. Neither scoring convention is substituted for the other.

## Method and evidence

Both decoders receive byte-identical seeded WAV files generated by Vail's
`cwgen`. The full grid uses the upstream case definitions, messages and four
CRC32-derived seeds. Default configuration, automatic channel selection and
native 8 kHz mono input are used without frequency or speed hints. libmorsedec
uses FFTW 3.3.10, rather than the DFT replacement in the upstream comparison.
The adapters feed four seconds of silence after each WAV; libmorsedec then calls
`md_flush()`. They score the final current-channel text. Normalization uppercases
text and collapses whitespace; word spacing, punctuation and O/0 differences
count as edits.

The additional 120 cases use three other messages, 14/19/27/34/39 WPM,
543/680/817 Hz, four noise conditions, timing jitter, fading and two seeds. They
were defined before evaluating that set, but subsequent development used their
results to diagnose word-spacing errors. They are additional validation data,
not an untouched holdout or an independent signal generator.

## Changes and tradeoffs

- Score candidate duration partitions by observed within-class log-duration
  residuals as well as their priors. Limit the influence of acquisition and
  inter-transmission silence on fitting, and correctly convert word-gap means
  to dot units when inferring missing classes.
- Keep learning through brief carrier dips. After roughly 0.8 seconds of
  sustained silence, permit short transmissions with at least three committed
  marks to produce text and qualify for automatic selection. This recovers
  `TEST` and `TU` without selecting isolated noise marks. Explicit flush also
  selects newly eligible short channels, including input ending on its last mark.
- Bound prominence at 200 so nearly noiseless long marks cannot drive the
  threshold arbitrarily high. Preserve uncapped amplitude for the SNR query.
- Blend tonal-confidence shoulders outward between learned speeds of 28 and
  34 WPM, allowing the broader spectra of short marks. The full timing estimate
  controls this blend; dot length alone harmed hand-keyed recordings.
- Preserve chronological order when splitting merged Morse patterns and
  normalize the word graph's root score along with every other hypothesis.
  The latter fixes missing spaces between generic words.

Uniformly loosening the confidence threshold and uniformly moving its shoulders
outward both improved synthetic CER but degraded the archived real recordings.
Those variants were rejected.

## Real recordings and verification

Using the existing archived references and chronological snapshot policy, the
three recordings changed from 13/2/9 errors to **10/2/7** errors: **19/170,
11.18% CER**, versus 24/170, 14.12%, before this work. These recordings were
already tuning material; their transcripts include partial characters and have
not been independently relabeled. They are a regression check, not a Vail
head-to-head measurement, since its final-current-channel policy differs from
the chronological assembly needed for QSO handoffs.

Final ASan/UBSan checks covered 8/44.1/48 kHz input, chunks of 1/85/997/4096
samples, and 5/10.625/20 ms hops at 8 kHz. Chunk sizes produced identical output
at each rate; all three hop cadences decoded the representative 20 WPM message.
Two simultaneous 20/30 WPM carriers at 650/950 Hz were both decoded, including
the weaker signal at −6 dB. Eight 31-second noise-only inputs, with and without
eight impulses/second, produced no current-channel text. Pattern splitting,
generic-word boundaries, malformed API input, non-finite samples, flush and
reset checks also passed. Short messages ending on the last mark decoded after
explicit flush, and pinning retained single-dot input. This is representative coverage, not exhaustive DSP
validation.

## CPU and memory

On an Intel i7-1165G7, GCC 15.2.0, optimized builds, a preloaded 39.929-second
8 kHz clip gave median streaming CPU times of **23.107 ms for libmorsedec** and
**20.551 ms for Vail**, approximately **1,728× and 1,943× real time**. These
measurements pin both processes to CPU 2 and alternate execution order over four
pairs of runs, each with nine repetitions. libmorsedec uses about 12% more CPU
on this workload; the accuracy improvement does not establish a speed advantage.
Earlier unpinned timings varied noticeably and are also archived. Construction, file I/O and
teardown are excluded; frontend and decoder processing, including any streaming
allocations, are included. Raw timings are archived in the validation report.

Memory policy is unchanged: libmorsedec preallocates its configured channel slots
to avoid allocation during streaming, while Vail allocates active channels as
needed. libmorsedec still uses more memory with its default 32-slot reservation;
these accuracy improvements do not claim memory parity. Hosts can reduce
`max_channels` when they need fewer simultaneous carriers.
